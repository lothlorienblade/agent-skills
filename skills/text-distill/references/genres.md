# Output forms by genre

Find the source's genre and follow its section. If the source is mixed (for example, a meeting where someone demonstrates a procedure), take the main form and add the sections you need from another. Templates are shown in English; write the document in the source's language.

## Contents

- Tutorial video, screen recording → step-by-step guide
- Lecture, monologue, podcast, webinar → notes
- Essay, article, post → thesis outline
- Interview, dialogue, conversation → summary by topic
- Meeting recording (Zoom, Teams, call) → minutes
- Messenger or email chat → summary of agreements
- Example: before → after

---

## Tutorial video, screen recording → step-by-step guide

Signs: "we click", "we go to", "we select"; the speaker shows an interface.

```
# <What we do> in <where>

## Before you start
- conditions, limits, what to prepare, minimum amounts and deadlines

## 1. <Stage>
1. Action. Where the element is → **Button name**.
2. …

## 2. <Stage>
…

## Result / next
- how to tell it worked; link to the next guide in the series
```

- One action per item. Write menu paths with "→".
- Put possible failures and how to get past them ("if error X appears, reload the page") next to the step where they happen.
- Variants for different cases ("in country A" / "elsewhere") get subheadings or a table, not a buried sentence.
- Form fields go in a table: "Field | What to enter".

## Lecture, monologue, podcast, webinar → notes

```
# <Topic>

## Key points
3–7 key theses, one sentence each.

## <Topic 1>
- thesis → argument, data, example (briefly)

## Terms (if there are many)
| Term | Meaning |

## Conclusions / recommendations
```

- Group by topic, not by the course of speech: if a point came up three times, gather it in one place.
- Shrink stories and examples to the one line that carries their meaning.

## Essay, article, post → thesis outline

```
# <Topic>

**Main idea:** one or two sentences.

## Arguments
1. Thesis. What supports it (fact, data, example).

## Counterarguments and limits (if the text has them)

## Conclusion
```

- The author's opinion stays an opinion: "the author argues that…". Do not turn judgments into facts.
- Drop rhetoric, metaphors, and literary digressions that carry no meaning.

## Interview, dialogue, conversation → summary by topic

```
# <What the conversation is about>

**Participants:** roles (Interviewer, Expert; or Participant 1, Participant 2)

## <Topic 1>
- Fact or position — (role, if it matters who said it)

## Disagreements (if any)

## Outcome
```

- Keep the question-and-answer form only when the format itself matters (an FAQ). Otherwise group by topic.
- Tell the participants' facts from their opinions.

## Meeting recording (Zoom, Teams, call) → minutes

```
# <Meeting topic>

**Date:** (if known) · **Participants:** roles

## Discussed
### <Item 1>
- key positions and arguments, briefly

## Decisions
1. …

## Tasks
| Task | Owner (role) | Due |

## Open questions
```

- Drop "can you hear me?", technical hiccups, small talk, and recaps for latecomers.
- A decision is what people agreed on. An item that was discussed but not closed goes to open questions.
- Convert relative deadlines ("by Friday", "next week") into dates when the meeting date is known, and check that weekday and date agree. Otherwise keep them as said and mention it in the report.

## Messenger or email chat → summary of agreements

```
# <Chat topic>

**Period:** dates · **Participants:** roles

## Current agreements
- the state of things at the end of the chat

## Tasks
| Task | Who | Due | Status |

## Key data
- links, files, non-personal details, figures

## Open questions
```

- Group by topic, not chronologically.
- When an agreement changed later, the last one wins. Mention the history only when it matters ("deadline moved from March 5 to March 12").
- Ignore stickers, "ok", "+", "thanks", service messages, and forwards without comment.
- Convert "tomorrow", "on Monday" into dates using the message date.

## Example: before → after

**Before** (excerpt from a tutorial video transcript):

> Hi everyone! John here, and today we're recording a guide on how to buy products. So, while you're in your personal account, you scroll down a bit. Here you can see how much time is left until the required purchase, at least 16 dollars. And the date itself, in my case it's September 30. We click "Go to store". […] If your connection is unstable, you might get a tab saying "Unable to access". Just reload the page and click "Place order" again. No big deal, it happens.

**After:**

```
## Before you start
- To keep the account active, make a purchase of at least **$16** before the date shown in the account.

## 1. Choose products
1. In your personal account, click **Go to store**.
…
4. Click **Place order**. If the error "Unable to access" appears, reload the page and click the button again.
```

What happened:
- The greeting, the name, and "no big deal" are gone.
- The presenter's own date (September 30) became a general rule.
- The $16 minimum stayed.
- The failure and how to get past it sit next to the step where it happens.
